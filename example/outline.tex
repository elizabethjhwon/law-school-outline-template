\chapter{Administrative Law}

\section{Agency Authority}

\rulebox{Delegation}{Congress may delegate authority when it supplies an intelligible principle to guide the agency's exercise of discretion.}

\definition{Intelligible principle}{The statutory standard that gives an agency meaningful direction while leaving room for administration and implementation.}

\casebox{Whitman v. American Trucking Ass'ns}{531 U.S. 457 (2001)}{The Court upheld the Clean Air Act's delegation to the EPA. The question is whether the statute provides an intelligible principle, not whether the agency has made every policy choice itself.}

\holding{Takeaway}{The nondelegation doctrine is real but highly deferential in modern practice.}

\analysisbox{How to analyze}{Identify the statutory grant, locate the limiting principle, and explain whether the agency's action stays within the authority Congress supplied.}

\policybox{Competing values}{Delegation promotes expertise and flexibility, but broad delegations raise accountability and separation-of-powers concerns.}

\subsection{Questions to ask}

\begin{itemize}
  \item What exactly did Congress authorize?
  \item What procedural or substantive limits appear in the statute?
  \item Is the challenge really about delegation, or about the agency's interpretation or implementation?
\end{itemize}

\examtip{On an exam, separate the constitutional delegation question from the statutory interpretation question.}

\section{Judicial Review}

\statutebox{Reviewability}{The Administrative Procedure Act generally creates a presumption that final agency action is reviewable unless a statute precludes review or the action is committed to agency discretion by law.}

\casebox{Loper Bright Enterprises v. Raimondo}{603 U.S. 369 (2024)}{Courts must exercise their independent judgment when deciding whether an agency has acted within its statutory authority. The statute's best reading controls, even when the statute is ambiguous.}

\question{Open issue}{How should a court distinguish independent judgment from appropriate respect for an agency's technical expertise?}
